## 1. General features, origin and functions (LO 1)

Connective tissue is defined by its matrix: the extracellular matrix (ECM) predominates over the cells (p. 980).

| Feature | Connective tissue | Contrast with epithelium |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cell packing | Not closely packed | Tightly packed |
| Polarity | None | Polarized |
| Attachment junctions | None | Present |
| Vascularity | Usually well vascularized | Avascular |
| Distribution | Widespread, except the central nervous system |  |

The epithelium column is from the Epithelium lecture, added for contrast.

| Origin fact | Detail (p. 980) |
| --- | --- |
| Germ layer | Mesoderm, as mesenchyme |
| Mesenchyme | Undifferentiated cells with minimal ECM |
| Role | Progenitor of all connective tissue cells |
| In the adult | Still present; replenishes the cells and is important in wound healing |

| Function | Provided by | Detail (p. 980) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supporting framework | Fibrous ECM | Capsules, trabeculae, and stroma that supports the parenchyma |
| Transport of nutrients | Amorphous ECM | A polyanionic hydrophilic gel |
| Defense against infection | Cells and amorphous ECM | Three steps, below |
| Wound healing | Fibroblasts, mesenchymal cells | Best capacity to regenerate |
| Storage | Adipose tissue |  |

The three defense steps, in order (p. 980):

1. Localization of the infection: more plasma leakage, higher extravascular osmotic pressure, fluid accumulation (edema)
2. Phagocytosis by blood leukocytes and connective tissue macrophages
3. Initiation of the immune response by lymphocytes, plasma cells and macrophages

| Capillary end | Hydrostatic pressure | Colloid osmotic pressure | Net fluid movement (pp. 971–972) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arterial | 40 mm Hg | 25 mm Hg | Out of the capillary |
| Venous | 15 mm Hg | 25 mm Hg | Back into the capillary |

Some of the fluid is drained by lymphatic capillaries instead (p. 972).

The trap in that table is the bone pair: the osteoblast is mesenchymal, the osteoclast is hematopoietic (fused monocytes, p. 973).

## 4. Ground substance (LO 3)

Ground substance is about 10% of the matrix by weight but fills about 90% of its volume (p. 985). The matrix is secreted mainly by fibroblasts.

| Component | What it is | Key facts | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Glycosaminoglycan (GAG) | Long unbranched chain of repeating disaccharides; one sugar is always an amino sugar | Binds large amounts of water; forms a polyanionic hydrated gel | 947, 985 |
| Proteoglycan | GAGs covalently bound to a central core protein; bottle-brush shape | More than 90% carbohydrate by weight | 949, 985 |
| Proteoglycan aggregate | Proteoglycans attached to hyaluronic acid by link proteins | Hyaluronan is the axis; the link protein joins the core protein to it | 948, 985 |
| Glycoprotein | Globular protein with short, branched monosaccharide chains | Mostly protein; promotes cell attachment to the matrix | 949, 985 |

The amino sugar is N-acetylglucosamine or N-acetylgalactosamine (p. 985). The hydrated gel allows rapid diffusion of water-soluble molecules and lets cells migrate during development and wound healing.

## 5. Collagen (LO 3)

Collagen is the major body protein, type I is the most common, and it stains with acid dyes, so it is eosinophilic (p. 987).

| Level | What it is | Page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tropocollagen | The basic unit: three alpha chains held by hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic interactions; 280 nm long | 986–987 |
| Fibril | Staggered tropocollagen units | 987 |
| Fiber | Bundled fibrils | 954 |
| Fiber bundle | Bundled fibers | 954 |

| Banding fact | Detail (pp. 986–987) |
| --- | --- |
| Period | 64 nm (640 Å), from the staggered arrangement |
| Dark bands | Gap (lacunar) regions between tropocollagen units |
| Why dark | The gaps fill with glycoproteins and proteoglycans, which are electron dense |
| Light bands | Overlapping regions with no gaps |

Hydroxyproline and hydroxylysine are the characteristic amino acids. Their hydroxylation depends on vitamin C and is essential for mechanical strength (p. 987).

Synthesis, in order (pp. 954, 989):

1. Nucleus: messenger RNA for each type of alpha chain
2. RER: alpha chains of preprocollagen made with registration peptides; signal peptide clipped
3. RER: hydroxylation of specific prolyl and lysyl residues (vitamin C dependent)
4. RER: galactosyl and glucosyl sugars attached to hydroxylysyl residues
5. Procollagen triple helix assembles
6. Golgi packages procollagen into secretory vesicles
7. Vesicles move to the cell surface along microtubules and microfilaments
8. Outside the cell: procollagen peptidases cleave the registration peptides, giving insoluble tropocollagen
9. Tropocollagen aggregates into fibrils
10. Lysyl oxidase makes covalent cross-links that reinforce the fibril

| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Most common | 987 |
| II | The cartilage collagen | 988 |
| III | Forms reticular fibers | 952, 990 |
| IV | Keeps its registration peptide, so no fibrils and no banding; laminin binds it | 985, 988 |
| VII | Long chains; attaches the basement membrane to the underlying stroma | 988 |
| IX and XI | Minor collagens with type II in cartilage; stabilize the type II network | 988 |

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

There are more than 20 collagen types (p. 953). Collagen recovered from a 68-million-year-old Tyrannosaurus rex bone was most similar to that of birds (pp. 960, 988).

## 7. What the lecture's own practice questions tested

The six practice questions at the end of the slides are faculty-written, so they show what he considers testable (pp. 973–978). Five of the six are image identification.

| Slide | What it asked | Answer on the slide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 973 | Which cell is derived from hematopoietic stem cells | Osteoclast (fused monocytes) |
| 974 | Identify the cell in an image | Plasma cell |
| 975 | Identify the cells in an image | Unilocular adipocytes |
| 976 | Identify the matrix component in an image | Collagen fibers |
| 977 | Identify the cell in an image | Macrophage |
| 978 | Classify the tissue in an image | Dense regular (tendon) |

One key to double-check: the slide 974 answer reads "E (abundant SER)", meaning smooth endoplasmic reticulum. The lecture slide and the handout both say the plasma cell has abundant rough endoplasmic reticulum (pp. 943, 983). The answer, plasma cell, is right either way. If an exam option forces the choice, go with rough, since two of his three sources say it.
