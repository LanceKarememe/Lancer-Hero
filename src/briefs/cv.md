# Cardiovascular Histology High-Yield Tables (Exam 3)

Cardiovascular System (Banerjee) was 23/32 on your September quiz, with capillaries the weakest part at 5/10. Page numbers are pages of the combined PDF: slides pp. 1079–1134, handout pp. 1135–1141. Everything is from those pages unless labeled (outside the slides).

## 1. The circuit and the vessel classes (LO 1)

Blood runs heart, elastic artery, muscular artery, arteriole, capillary, venule, small to medium vein, large vein, heart (pp. 1095, 1135).

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Lymphatic | Lymph | No pump | One direction only | 1084 |

| Vessel | Definition on the slide | Page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Artery | Carries blood away from the heart | 1085 |
| Vein | Returns blood to the heart | 1085 |
| Capillary | Connects the arterial side to the venous side, with special exceptions | 1085 |

| Type of microcirculation | Route | Where | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Usual | Arteriole, capillary bed, venule | Most tissues | 1107 |

The handout names two portal systems: the hepatic portal system (gut capillaries to liver sinusoids) and the hypophyseal portal system (hypothalamus capillaries down the pituitary stalk to the adenohypophysis) (p. 1136).

## 2. Capillaries (LO 1–2)

Capillaries and post-capillary venules are the exchange vessels: gases, metabolic waste, nutrients and hormones (p. 1087).

| Part of the wall | Fact | Page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Endothelium | Single layer; nuclei bulge into the lumen | 1088, 1136 |
| Basal lamina | Thin | 1088 |
| Pericyte | Wraps the outside of the endothelium; helps maintain endothelial cell viability | 1088 |
| Diameter | About 7–9 microns, just wide enough for one red cell | 1088, 1136 |
| Hallmark under the electron microscope | Many pinocytotic pits and vesicles, about 80 nm | 1136 |

The handout says the pericyte's function is not clear and that it may become smooth muscle or a phagocytic cell (p. 1136). If a question quotes the slide, answer "maintains endothelial viability".

| Capillary type | Wall | Where | How to recognize it | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Continuous | No pores; pinocytotic vesicles | Muscle, skin, lung, brain (blood-brain barrier), connective tissue | Unbroken thin wall | 1089–1090, 1136 |
| Fenestrated | Pores, each spanned by a thin diaphragm | Intestine, endocrine glands | Wall thins to regularly spaced openings | 1089–1090, 1136 |
| Fenestrated, kidney | Pores with NO diaphragm; thick basement membrane | Renal glomerulus | Open pores over a thick basal lamina | 1089, 1136 |
| Sinusoid (discontinuous) | Gaps between endothelial cells; basal lamina missing or discontinuous; up to 30–40 microns wide, irregular lumen | Liver, spleen, bone marrow | Wide, irregular channel | 1089–1090, 1136 |
| Fenestrated sinusoid | Large fenestrated capillary | Adrenal gland, adenohypophysis | Wide and fenestrated | 1089, 1136 |

Kupffer cells sit inside the lumen of liver sinusoids (p. 1090).

| Exchange mechanism | What crosses | Detail | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Diffusion | Gases and lipid-soluble substances | Straight across the plasmalemma | 1137 |
| Filtration | Water and small molecules | Through "leaky tight junctions", driven by hydrostatic versus oncotic pressure | 1137 |
| Vesicular transport (transcytosis) | Large molecules over 9 nm, such as plasma proteins | The main mechanism for large molecules; pinocytotic pits and vesicles; not energy dependent | 1091, 1137 |
| Transendothelial channels | Molecules under 9 nm | Formed by fusion of vesicles; spanned by diaphragms | 1137 |

The slide says transendothelial channels carry both small and large molecules (p. 1092); the handout limits them to under 9 nm (p. 1137). Answer by the handout unless the question quotes the slide.

| Endothelial cell function | Detail (p. 1093) |
| --- | --- |

## 3. The three tunics (LO 2)

Every larger vessel has the same three coats; arteries and veins differ in which coat is thick (pp. 1097–1098).

| Tunic | Contains | Arteries | Veins |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Intima | Endothelium, subendothelial connective tissue, internal elastic lamina (IEL) | | |
| Media | Smooth muscle, elastic fibers, external elastic lamina (EEL) | Thicker | Thinner |
| Adventitia | Connective tissue, blood vessels (vasa vasorum), nerves | Thinner | Thicker |

| Compared with its companion artery, a vein has (p. 1139) |
| --- |
| Less muscle |
| More connective tissue |
| Less elastic tissue |
| A larger lumen |
| Valves, in the extremities |

## 4. The vessel table (LO 2)

This is his table on p. 1100, and his practice question (pp. 1128–1129) is answered straight from the smooth muscle column: count the layers of smooth muscle nuclei in the media.

| Vessel | Lumen | Intima | Smooth muscle in media | Adventitia | Elastic lamina |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arteriole | Under 100 microns | Thin | 1–5 layers | Thin | May or may not have internal |
| Muscular (distributing) artery | About 300 microns | Thin | Up to 40 layers | Thin | Internal and external |
| Elastic artery (aorta) | Large | Thick | 40–60 layers | Thin | 40–60 throughout the media |
| Collecting venule | Under 50 microns | Thin | None | Thin | None |
| Muscular venule | About 100–200 microns | Thin | 1–2 layers | Thin | None |
| Small to medium vein | About 200 to over 1000 microns | Thin, with valves | Thin or absent | Thick | Internal, vague |
| Large vein (vena cava) | Large | Thick | Thin, up to several layers | Thick, with many longitudinal smooth muscle bundles | Internal |

| How to recognize it in an image | Clue | Page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Elastic artery | Media packed with wavy elastic sheets; with an elastic stain, many dark wavy lines | 1103 |
| Muscular artery | Scalloped dark internal elastic lamina just under the lining; thick muscular media | 1104–1105 |
| Arteriole | Small round vessel, wall 1–5 muscle cells thick, lumen about as wide as the wall | 1106 |
| Venule or vein | Thin wall, large collapsed or blood-filled lumen, next to a thicker-walled artery | 1109–1112 |
| Large vein | Thin media; adventitia holds longitudinal smooth muscle bundles cut in cross-section, and vasa vasorum | 1113 |
| Valve | Thin flap of intima projecting into a vein lumen; keeps flow toward the heart | 1114 |

Order of flow through the small vessels: arteriole, capillary, post-capillary venule, collecting venule, muscular venule (p. 1111).

## 5. The heart (LO 3)

The heart wall is three layers built on a cardiac skeleton of dense collagenous connective tissue, and each layer matches a vessel tunic (pp. 1117, 1139).

| Heart layer | Homologous to | Made of | How to recognize it | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Endocardium | Tunica intima | Endothelium on a thin connective tissue layer; thicker subendocardial connective tissue beneath | The lining of the chamber; Purkinje fibers lie just under it | 1119, 1141 |
| Myocardium | Tunica media | Cardiac muscle; thin in atria, much thicker in ventricles; attached to the fibrous skeleton | The bulk of the wall | 1141 |
| Epicardium (visceral pericardium) | Tunica adventitia | Mesothelium on the surface; subepicardial loose connective tissue with fat, vessels and nerves | Outer surface with adipose tissue | 1119, 1141 |

| Muscle | Shape | Nuclei | Striated | Where | Control | 
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cardiac | Branched networks | Single | Yes, with intercalated discs | Walls of the heart | Involuntary |
| Smooth | Tapered at each end | Single | No | Walls of hollow visceral organs | Involuntary |
| Skeletal | Long and cylindrical | Many | Yes | Attached to bones | Voluntary |

That table is p. 1101.

| Purkinje fiber fact | Detail | Page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | A specialized cardiac muscle cell | 1120 |
| Speed | Conducts impulses 5 times faster than surrounding cells | 1120 |
| Look | Large, pale cells under the endocardium | 1119, 1131 |
| Result | Gap junctions let the wave of depolarization start contraction at the apex | 1120 |

The pericardial cavity is a potential space between visceral and parietal pericardium holding up to 50 mL of fluid (p. 1141). Before fish, cephalochordates had no heart, only muscular vessels (p. 1116).

## 6. Lymphatics and clinical points

Lymphatic vessels return fluid and plasma protein from the interstitium to the blood (p. 1141).

| Lymphatic function | Page |
| --- | --- |
| Return the excess 10% of interstitial fluid to the blood | 1122 |
| Transport lipids absorbed from the intestine | 1122 |
| Immunologic support | 1122 |

| Lymph capillary feature | Detail | Page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start | End blindly | 1141 |
| Wall | A single layer of endothelium only | 1141 |
| Basal lamina | Discontinuous or missing | 1141 |
| Pericytes | None | 1141 |
| Anchoring | Filaments (elastic fibers) tie the endothelium to the surrounding connective tissue | 1124, 1141 |

| Larger lymph vessel versus vein | Detail (p. 1141) |
| --- | --- |
| Valves | Many |
| Wall | Thinner than a vein |
| Contents | No red cells, only lymphocytes |

All lymph is filtered by at least one lymph node before it re-enters the blood, through the thoracic duct and the right lymphatic duct (pp. 1125, 1141).
