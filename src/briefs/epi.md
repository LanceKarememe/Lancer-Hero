## 1. The three microscopes (LO 1)

The three instruments differ in what carries the image (light, transmitted electrons, or secondary electrons), and that sets the magnification and resolution.

| Feature | Light microscope (LM) | Transmission electron microscope (TEM) | Scanning electron microscope (SEM) | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| How it focuses | Glass lenses focus light | Magnets focus a beam of electrons | Electromagnetic lenses plus a scan generator | 91, 92, 197 |
| What forms the image | Transmitted light | Transmitted primary electrons on a fluorescent screen | Secondary electrons collected by a signal detector, shown on a cathode ray tube | 195, 197, 199 |
| Magnification | Up to 1,500 | Up to 500,000 | Up to 100,000 | 91, 92, 93 |
| Resolution | 0.2 micron | 1 to 1.5 nm | 2 to 10 nm | 194, 196, 197 |
| Picture | 2 dimensional, in colour | 2 dimensional, black and white | 3 dimensional surface | 199 |
| Same kidney tubule | Pink cells, fuzzy lumen edge | Individual microvilli and organelles | Carpeted inner surface of a broken tubule | 94, 95, 96 |

| Shared by both electron microscopes | Page |
| --- | --- |
| Vacuum pump, so electrons can flow | 195, 197 |
| Electron source is a tungsten filament | 195, 197 |

| Unit | Size | Use | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Micrometer | 10^-6 m; a red cell is 7 to 9 | Light microscope | 190 |
| Nanometer (nm) | 10^-9 m; plasma membrane is 7.5 to 10 | Electron microscope | 190 |
| Angstrom | 10^-10 m | Older electron microscope unit | 190 |

Resolution is the minimum distance at which two close structures can still be told apart, and a higher numerical aperture gives better resolution (p. 194).

| Type of light microscopy | What it is | Page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brightfield | Light shines through a stained specimen; the most common | 194 |
| Fluorescence | Ultraviolet light excites the specimen to emit visible light | 194 |
| Phase contrast | Good for living and unstained specimens, such as cultured cells | 194 |

One small conflict: the slide says SEM magnifies "up to 100,000 times" (p. 93) and the handout says "in excess of 100,000" (p. 197). Answer 100,000 either way.

## 2. Preparing tissue, and the stains (LO 2)

Every method fixes and dehydrates; they differ in what supports the tissue and what gives contrast.

| Step | LM | TEM | SEM | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fixation | Formalin (cross-links proteins) | Glutaraldehyde (proteins, nucleic acids), then osmium tetroxide (lipids) | Same as TEM | 191, 195, 197 |
| Dehydration | Ethanol (alcohol or acetone) | Ethanol | Ethanol | 97, 109, 124 |
| Support | Paraffin embedding | Plastic (Epon), then polymerized | None; critical point drying avoids drying artifacts | 98, 110, 197 |
| Sectioning | Microtome, steel knife, 2 to 10 micrometers | Ultramicrotome, glass or diamond knife, 20 to 100 nm | None | 191, 195 |
| Mounting | Glass slide | Wire mesh grid | Glued to a metal stub | 191, 195, 197 |
| Contrast | Coloured stains | Heavy metals: uranyl acetate, lead citrate | Gold or palladium coat, to enhance electron emission | 195, 197 |
| Preparation time | 4 to 5 days | 2 to 3 days | 5 to 8 hours | 199 |

Three conflicts to know. (1) Fixative: the slides list formalin for TEM and SEM as well (pp. 109, 124), while the handout says glutaraldehyde then osmium tetroxide (p. 195) and "same as TEM" for SEM (p. 197). Answer his handout wording unless a question quotes the slide. (2) TEM section thickness: the handout text says 20 to 100 nm (p. 195) but its Table 1 says 100 to 600 angstroms, which is 10 to 60 nm (p. 199). Use 20 to 100 nm. (3) Xylene: the slide puts it under dehydration (p. 97), the handout's Figure 4 calls it clearing (p. 192).

## 3. Freeze fracture: P-face versus E-face (LO 2)

Freeze fracture splits a membrane down the middle of its bilayer, so you look at the inside of each half, never at the true surface (p. 196).

| Step | Detail | Page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Physical fixation by rapid freezing (liquid nitrogen or freon); no chemical fixative | 199 |
| 2 | Fracture under high vacuum; the plane follows the hydrophobic middle of the membrane | 196, 199 |
| 3 | Coat with platinum and carbon to make a metal replica | 199 |
| 4 | Digest the cells away; view the replica in the TEM | 196, 199 |

| Face | Which half of the bilayer | Particles | On his slide | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P-face | Inner half, the half next to the cytoplasm | Most of the integral protein particles | The heavily studded side | 123, 196 |
| E-face | Outer half, the half next to the outside of the cell | Few particles; smoother | The finer, emptier side | 123, 196 |

The handout sentence that confused you: "the E-face, the inwardly facing outer half, and the P-face, the outwardly facing inner half" (p. 196). Read it in two parts. "Outer half" and "inner half" say which leaflet you have. "Inwardly facing" and "outwardly facing" only say which way the newly exposed surface points: the outer leaflet's fractured surface looks in toward the cell, and the inner leaflet's fractured surface looks out. So: P-face = inner half = many particles. E-face = outer half = few particles. Memory aid, P for protoplasm and E for extracellular (outside the slides).

## 4. Four basic tissues, and what makes an epithelium (LO 3)

There are four basic tissues, and glandular is not one of them: glands are epithelium (pp. 129, 184).

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Connective | Several types of fixed and wandering cells | Abundant | Support and protection |
| Muscle | Elongated contractile cells | Moderate | Movement |
| Nervous | Intertwining elongated processes | None | Transmission of nervous impulses |

## 6. Cell junctions (LO 8)

From the apex down the order is tight junction, zonula adherens, desmosome, with gap junctions lower and hemidesmosomes on the basal surface (pp. 144, 153).

| Junction | Other name | Shape and place | Transmembrane proteins | Filaments | Job | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Zonula occludens | Tight junction | Band around the apical end | Claudin, occludin | | Barrier: stops molecules passing between cells; keeps polarity | 145, 146 |
| Zonula adherens | Intermediate junction, adhesion belt | Band just below the tight junction | Cadherins (bind catenin) | Actin | Anchors cells to each other; its actin forms the terminal web | 149 |
| Desmosome | Macula adherens | "Spot welds" around the cell | Desmoglein, desmocollin (cadherin family); plaque of plakoglobin and desmoplakin | Keratin | Attaches cells to each other | 153, 154 |
| Hemidesmosome | Half-desmosome | Basal surface | Integrins (replace cadherins) | Keratin | Anchors the cell to the basal lamina; in high-stress tissue such as skin | 153, 155, 205 |
| Gap junction | Communicating junction | Patches on lateral membrane | Six connexins make a connexon; two connexons make a channel | | Small molecules (sodium, potassium, chloride) pass through channels under 1.5 nm; rapid signalling, rhythmic contraction | 158, 159 |

## 9. Glands: exocrine versus endocrine (LO 5)

Both kinds grow down from a covering epithelium; exocrine glands keep the duct and endocrine glands lose it (pp. 163, 210).

## 11. Modes of secretion (LO 7)
