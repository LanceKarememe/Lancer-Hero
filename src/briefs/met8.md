## 1. Nitrogen balance and essential amino acids (LO 1)

Nitrogen balance is the difference between nitrogen (protein) intake and excretion, and nine amino acids must come from the diet (p. 877, p. 878).

**Table 1a. The three states of nitrogen balance (p. 877)**

| State | Definition | When it happens |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | Intake exceeds excretion | Growth |
| Positive | Intake exceeds excretion | Recovery from injury |
| Neutral | Intake equals excretion | Normal situation for many healthy adults |
| Negative | Excretion exceeds intake | Weight loss |
| Negative | Excretion exceeds intake | Starvation |
| Negative | Excretion exceeds intake | Ketoacidosis |

**Table 1b. The nitrogen pool (p. 877)**

| Connected to the amino acid pool | Direction of the arrow |
|---|---|

| Other new amino acids | Both ways |
| Non-protein compounds: heme, heterocyclic amines | Both ways |
| Glucose, glycogen, lipids | Out only |

## 5. Methionine and folate cycles, one-carbon metabolism (LO 5)

The methionine cycle makes S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), the folate cycle carries single carbons on tetrahydrofolate (THF), and vitamin B12 joins them at methionine synthase (p. 886, p. 887).

**Table 5a. The three arms of single carbon metabolism (p. 886)**

| Arm | Key molecule | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Methionine cycle | Key product: S-adenosylmethionine | Universal methyl donor used in more than 200 pathways |
| Folate cycle | Key intermediate: tetrahydrofolate | Single carbon carrier |
| Transsulfuration shunt | Key product: glutathione (and cysteine) | The cell's master antioxidant |

**Table 5b. What each arm is needed for (p. 886)**

|---|---|

| Methionine cycle | Synthesis of lipids |

| Folate cycle | Passing methyl groups to the methionine cycle |
| Folate cycle | Rapid cell division: embryological development, red blood cell production |
| Transsulfuration shunt | Neutralizing oxidative stress |
| Transsulfuration shunt | Neutralizing heavy metals |
