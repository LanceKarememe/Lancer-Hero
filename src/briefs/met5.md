## 3. Fatty acyl-CoA, the carnitine shuttle and beta-oxidation (LO 3)

Fatty acids are activated to fatty acyl-coenzyme A (CoA), carried into the matrix on carnitine, and cut into acetyl-CoA by beta-oxidation (p. 784).

**Overall order of fatty acid oxidation (p. 784)**

1. Convert the free fatty acid to fatty acyl-CoA.
2. Transport it into the mitochondria by the carnitine shuttle.
3. Carry out repeated rounds of beta-oxidation.
4. Odd-chain and unsaturated fatty acids require additional steps, which she does not show.

**Three stages for a 16-carbon fatty acid (p. 784)**

| Stage | Process | What the slide shows it producing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Beta-oxidation | 8 acetyl-CoA, plus electrons |
| 2 | Citric acid cycle | 16 carbon dioxide, plus 64 electrons |
| 3 | Respiratory (electron-transfer) chain | Adenosine triphosphate (ATP) from adenosine diphosphate (ADP) and phosphate, and water from oxygen |

Electrons from stages 1 and 2 are carried as NADH and FADH2.

| Carrier | Full name | Oxidized form |
|---|---|---|
| NADH | Reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide | NAD+ |
| FADH2 | Reduced flavin adenine dinucleotide | FAD |

**Step 1 of the overall order: making fatty acyl-CoA (p. 785)**

| Step | Enzyme | What happens | Standard free energy change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fatty acyl-CoA synthetase | ATP adenylylates the carboxylate ion, giving enzyme-bound fatty acyl-adenylate and pyrophosphate | |
| 2 | Fatty acyl-CoA synthetase | The thiol group of CoA attacks the acyl-adenylate, displacing adenosine monophosphate (AMP) and forming the thioester fatty acyl-CoA | -15 kJ/mol for the two-step process |
| Side reaction | Inorganic pyrophosphatase | Pyrophosphate is immediately hydrolyzed to two molecules of phosphate | -19 kJ/mol |

**Step 2 of the overall order: the carnitine shuttle (p. 786)**

| Step | What happens | Enzyme or protein | Location as drawn |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Acyl group moves from CoA to carnitine, releasing free CoA | Carnitine acyltransferase I | Outer mitochondrial membrane, facing the cytosol |
| 2 | Acylcarnitine enters the matrix and free carnitine returns | Transporter | Inner mitochondrial membrane |
| 3 | Acyl group moves from carnitine back to CoA, giving fatty acyl-CoA | Carnitine acyltransferase II | Matrix side of the inner membrane |

| Why the shuttle exists | Page |
|---|---|
| Cells keep separate pools of CoA in the cytoplasm and matrix | 786 |
| Fatty acyl-CoA therefore cannot be directly imported into the matrix | 786 |
| In the cytoplasm the fatty acid is attached to carnitine | 786 |
| Carnitine acts like a "handle" that helps carry the fatty acid into the matrix | 786 |
| Once in the matrix, the fatty acid is reattached to CoA | 786 |

## 4. Ketone bodies, fasting and diabetic ketoacidosis (LO 4)

The liver turns excess acetyl-coenzyme A (CoA) into ketone bodies whenever gluconeogenesis drains the cycle, and peripheral tissues burn them (p. 791).

**The three ketone bodies (p. 791, 792)**

| Ketone body | Note on the slides | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Acetone | Exhaled | 792 |
| Acetoacetate | Released into blood and used by peripheral tissues | 791, 792 |
| D-beta-hydroxybutyrate | Released into blood and used by peripheral tissues | 791, 792 |

**Why the liver makes ketone bodies (p. 791)**

1. Gluconeogenesis and ketone synthesis occur together.
2. Gluconeogenesis draws off oxaloacetate and malate from the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle.
3. This can cause a bottleneck at the first reaction of the TCA cycle.
4. Ketone bodies are formed from the excess acetyl-CoA and released into the blood.

| Function of ketone body release | Page |
|---|---|
| Avoids inhibition of continued beta-oxidation by high acetyl-CoA (thiolase is the enzyme inhibited) | 791, 807 |
| Shares the energy stored in the fatty acid with the extra-hepatic tissues | 791 |

**Ketogenesis in order (p. 792)**

| 4 | HMG-CoA to acetoacetate | HMG-CoA lyase | Mitochondria of liver cells |
| 5 | Acetoacetate to beta-hydroxybutyrate, using reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NADH) | | Mitochondria of liver cells |

## 6. Anaplerotic reactions (LO 6)

**The four reactions (p. 806)**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pyruvate carboxylase | Pyruvate | Bicarbonate and adenosine triphosphate (ATP) | Oxaloacetate, plus adenosine diphosphate (ADP) and phosphate | Liver, kidney |

| PEP carboxylase | PEP | Bicarbonate | Oxaloacetate, plus phosphate | Higher plants, yeast, bacteria |
| Malic enzyme | Pyruvate | Bicarbonate and reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide, with or without phosphate, written NAD(P)H | Malate, plus NAD(P)+ | Widely distributed in eukaryotes and bacteria |
