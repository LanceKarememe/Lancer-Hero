# Hematopoiesis High-Yield Tables (Exam 3)

Hematopoiesis (Djakiew) was 15/30 on your September quiz, with granulopoiesis at 0/4. Page numbers are pages of the combined PDF: slides pp. 446–494, handout pp. 495–507. Everything is from those pages unless labeled (outside the slides).

## 1. Where blood is made, fetus to adult (LO 1)

Hematopoiesis moves through three sites before birth and ends in the bone marrow, which is the only site at term (pp. 450, 495).

| Stage | Site | When it dominates | What it makes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primordial | Yolk sac | Earliest embryo | Large, nucleated red cells (primordial erythroblasts) |
| Hepato-spleno-thymic | Liver, spleen, thymus | Second trimester | Granulocyte and megakaryocyte precursors; definitive erythroblasts |
| Medullo-lymphatic | Bone marrow and lymph nodes | Third trimester; exclusive at term | All cell types |

Extramedullary hematopoiesis is the return of blood production to the liver and spleen after prolonged, severe demand in a child or adult (p. 495).

| --- | --- | --- |
| At birth | Every bone cavity, completely full of cells | 497 |
| Adult | Only 50% of marrow is active | 497 |
| Adult sites | Ribs, sternum, pelvis; proximal quarter of humerus and femur | 497 |
| On the slide's curve | Axial skeleton stays active; distal long bones fall off by about age 20 | 450 |

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Red | Hematogenous tissue; highly vascular, soft | Active (young) | 455, 497 |

Red and yellow marrow are interconvertible as demand changes (p. 499).

## 2. Stem cells and the two branches (LO 2)

One pluripotential stem cell makes every blood cell, and its first split is into a myeloid line and a lymphoid line (pp. 453, 495).

| Branch | Stays or leaves | Makes | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Myeloid | Develops in the bone marrow | Erythrocytes, megakaryocytes and platelets, granulocytes (neutrophil, eosinophil, basophil), monocytes | 453, 501 |
| Lymphoid | Generally migrates to lymphoid organs | T lymphocytes (cellular immunity), B lymphocytes (humoral immunity) | 453, 501 |

Monocytes that leave the blood become macrophages (p. 453).

## 3. Bone marrow structure (LO 3)

Marrow has a vascular compartment of sinusoids and an extravascular hematopoietic compartment between them (p. 497).

| Part | What it is | Page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Endosteum | The outer capsule of the marrow | 497 |
| Venous sinusoids | Thin-walled; continuous endothelium with a discontinuous basement membrane | 497 |
| Stromal cells (adventitial reticular cells) | Framework of cells plus reticular fibers; cover the outer surface of the sinusoid | 457, 497 |
| Hematopoietic inductive microenvironment | Stromal cells, adhesion molecules and growth factors; directs differentiation and release | 495, 497 |
| Macrophages | Remove debris and dead cells | 499 |

Stem cells are "seeds in the soil": they need that microenvironment to develop, and they can home back to it (pp. 495, 497).

| Lineage | Where it sits (handout) | How it reaches the blood | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Erythroid | Near the sinuses, around a nurse macrophage (erythroblastic islet) | Passes through the endothelium | 498 |
| Granulocytes | Away from the sinuses | Motile metamyelocytes move toward the sinus | 498 |
| Megakaryocytes | On the outer (adventitial) surface of the sinus | Push proplatelets through the endothelium into the lumen | 499, 507 |
| Lymphocytes | B cells mature in marrow; T cells mature in the thymus |  | 499 |

One conflict to know about: the textbook figure on slide 458 says developing granulocytes are found adjacent to venous sinusoids, while his handout says granulopoiesis is found away from the sinuses. The handout is his own wording, so answer "away from the sinuses" unless a question quotes the figure.

Aplastic anemia is pancytopenia with a fatty, "empty", hypocellular marrow (p. 499).

## 4. Erythropoiesis (LO 3)

Across the red cell line the cell shrinks, the cytoplasm goes from blue to pink, and the nucleus condenses and is thrown out (pp. 462, 1154).

| Stage | Cytoplasm | Nucleus | Mitotic | How to spot it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Proerythroblast | Basophilic | Homogeneous chromatin | Yes | Largest; first recognizable erythroid cell |
| Basophilic erythroblast | Basophilic; RNA-rich for protein synthesis | Checkerboard heterochromatin | Yes | Deep blue rim around a checkerboard nucleus |
| Polychromatophilic erythroblast | Basophilic and eosinophilic together |  | Yes | Gray or mixed-color cytoplasm |
| Orthochromatophilic erythroblast (normoblast) | Eosinophilic | Condensed (pyknotic), eccentric | No | Small dark off-center nucleus; cell about red-cell size |
| Reticulocyte | Residual RNA | None | No | Blue web with a supravital stain |
| Erythrocyte | Uniform eosinophilic | None | No |  |

The descriptions come from his review lecture (p. 1154), the faculty answer on p. 491, and the handout list (p. 503). "Gray" and "deep blue rim" are my wording for the same facts.

## 5. Granulopoiesis (LO 3)

Two things change down the granulocyte line: which granules are being made, and the shape of the nucleus (pp. 469–472, 505, 1155).

| Stage | Granules | Nucleus | Mitotic | Amoeboid movement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Myeloblast | None | Round | Yes | No |
| Promyelocyte | Azurophilic (non-specific, primary) granules first made | Round or oval | Yes | No |
| Myelocyte | Specific (secondary) granules first made | Round or oval, often eccentric | Yes | No |
| Metamyelocyte | No new granules; holds both kinds | Bean shaped | No | Begins |
| Band (stab) cell | Both kinds | Horseshoe shaped | No | Yes |
| Mature granulocyte | Both kinds | Segmented | No | Yes |

Non-specific granules are lysosomes (p. 1155).

Band neutrophils accumulating in the blood indicate bacterial infection (p. 474). Recombinant GM-CSF helps patients with a low white cell count after marrow transplant, cancer therapy, AIDS or aplastic anemia (p. 503).

## 6. Monocytes and platelets (LO 3)

Monocytes share a stem cell with neutrophils, and platelets are fragments of the largest cell in the marrow (pp. 476, 505).

| Monocyte stage | Detail | Page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CFU-GM | Shared restricted stem cell | 505 |
| Monoblast | Virtually identical to a myeloblast | 505 |
| Promonocyte | First morphologically identifiable cell | 505 |
| Monocyte | About 6 days in marrow, then a blood half-time of 1–3 days | 476 |

## 8. Faculty questions on the slides, and your September misses

The five practice questions at the end are faculty-written, and three of them ask what comes before the cell shown (pp. 489–493). Learn each line as an ordered list, not as separate pictures.

| Slide | What it asked | Answer on the slide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 489 | Which cell is not from a myeloid progenitor | Lymphocyte |
| 490 | Which cell precedes the one shown (a polychromatophilic erythroblast) | Basophilic erythroblast |
| 491 | Identify the cell from erythropoiesis | Normoblast: pyknotic, eccentric nucleus; cell about red-cell size |
| 492 | Which cell precedes the one shown (a band cell) | Metamyelocyte |
| 493 | Which cell does not contain specific granules | Promyelocyte |

The review lecture adds two more: a polychromatophilic erythroblast identified by its polychromatophilia (p. 1172), and a metamyelocyte identified by its kidney-bean nucleus (p. 1173).

The two orders to say out loud:

1. Proerythroblast, basophilic erythroblast, polychromatophilic erythroblast, normoblast, reticulocyte, erythrocyte
2. Myeloblast, promyelocyte, myelocyte, metamyelocyte, band cell, mature granulocyte
