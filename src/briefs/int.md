## 2. Epidermal layers (LO 1)

Thick skin has five layers, listed here from deep to superficial; thin skin lacks the stratum lucidum (pp. 1000, 1006, 1069).

| Layer | Cells | Key content | Mitosis | How to recognize it in an image | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stratum basale (germinativum) | Single layer of cuboidal or columnar stem cells | Desmosomes to neighbors; hemidesmosomes to the basement membrane | Intense | One row of crowded dark nuclei sitting on the dermis | 1001, 1069 |
| Stratum spinosum | Layers of cuboidal or polygonal cells, central nuclei | Tonofibrils (bundles of cytokeratin filaments) inserting into desmosomes; active protein synthesis | Low | Polygonal cells joined by fine "prickles" across a pale gap | 1002, 1003, 1065, 1069 |
| Stratum granulosum | Flattened cells | Keratohyalin granules, lamellar granules, tonofibrils | None | Flat cells packed with dark granules, just under the pale dead layers | 1004, 1069 |
| Stratum lucidum | Dead; organelles digested | Cytokeratin aggregates in a keratohyalin (filaggrin) matrix | None | Thin clear band; thick skin only | 1005, 1069 |
| Stratum corneum | Squames: flat dead cells | Mature keratin; abundant intercellular lipid | None | Outermost flakes with no nuclei | 1005, 1069 |

The prickles of the stratum spinosum are an artifact of tissue shrinkage: cells pull apart but stay joined at their desmosomes (p. 1002). The lucidum and corneum are the non-living layers (p. 1005). Squames are shed as their desmosomes are lost (p. 1069).

| Granule of the stratum granulosum | Membrane | Content | Job | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Keratohyalin granule | None | Mainly filaggrin, which binds keratin; very basophilic (many phosphate groups) | Bundles keratin | 1004, 1069 |
| Lamellar granule | Membrane coated | Lipid sheets | Secreted between cells: intercellular cement and a barrier to desiccation | 1004, 1069 |

| Function of the epidermis | How | Page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Abrasion resistance | Loose squames of the stratum corneum flake off; tonofibrils and desmosomes of the spinosum | 1008, 1069 |
| Physical-chemical barrier | Blocks infectious agents and most substances | 1008, 1069 |
| Water proofing | Intercellular lipid from lamellar granules | 1008, 1069 |
| Vitamin D | Photo biogenesis with ultraviolet (UV) light | 1069 |
| Cytokines | Keratinocytes make interleukins | 1069 |

Substances that do cross the barrier: poison ivy resins, nitroglycerin, scopolamine, DMSO (p. 1069).

## 3. Junctions and the dermal-epidermal junction (LO 1)

Cell-to-cell is desmosome and cadherin; cell-to-basement-membrane is hemidesmosome and integrin (pp. 1001, 1025).

| Junction | Joins | Protein | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Desmosome | Keratinocyte to keratinocyte | Cadherins; the core proteins are desmogleins | 1001, 1067 |
| Hemidesmosome | Basal cell to basement membrane | Integrins | 1001, 1025 |

Keratin intermediate filaments anchor to desmosomes; desmosomes give mechanical strength, resist stretching, and are broken and reformed as cells move up (pp. 998, 1067).

| Layer of the junction, top to bottom | Main component | Page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lamina lucida | Laminins | 1025, 1072 |
| Lamina densa | Type IV collagen | 1025, 1072 |
| Fibroreticular lamina | Fibronectin | 1025, 1072 |
| Anchoring fibrils | Type VII collagen; found nowhere else in the body | 1025, 1072 |

| Junction fact | Detail | Page |

## 4. The four epidermal cell types (LO 2)

Keratinocytes make up most of the epidermis; the other three are scattered among them (pp. 997, 1067).

| Cell | Origin | Where | Job | Key fact | How to recognize it in an image | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Keratinocyte | Ectoderm | Every layer | Makes keratin | Linked by desmosomes; makes interleukins | The ordinary cell of each layer | 998, 1067 |
| Melanocyte | Neural crest | Stratum basale; also hair follicles, eye | Makes melanin, which blocks UV and soaks up free radicals | Feeds a group of keratinocytes: the epidermal-melanin unit | Basal cell body with long processes running up between keratinocytes | 1012, 1070 |
| Langerhans cell | Bone marrow (from monocytes) | Mainly stratum spinosum | Antigen presenting cell (APC): processes skin antigens and presents them to lymphocytes | No cytokeratin filaments, no desmosomes; travels to lymph nodes by dermal lymphatics | Star-shaped (dendritic) cells in the mid epidermis; stained brown on p. 1061 | 1016, 1061, 1071 |
| Merkel cell | Neural crest | Stratum basale, especially thick skin | Sensory | Dense core (neurotransmitter) granules make it an APUD cell; nerve ending at its base is the Merkel's disk | Clear basal cell with a nerve ending against its base; small dark granules on the electron-microscope drawing | 1018, 1071 |

Langerhans cells belong to the mononuclear phagocyte system (MPS) (p. 1071). APUD is not spelled out on these pages (outside the slides: amine precursor uptake and decarboxylation). Certain T lymphocytes also home to the epidermis, drawn by keratinocyte interleukins (p. 1071).

Two small conflicts on the Merkel cell. The slide calls it sensory and says it is contacted exclusively by myelinated axons (p. 1018); the handout says its function is unclear (mechanoreceptor or neuro-endocrine) and that free nerve endings form the disk (p. 1071). Answer the handout wording unless a question quotes the slide. The slide also calls its tumor rare (p. 1018).

## 10. Skin cancers (LO 2)

Each cancer is named for the cell it starts from (pp. 1019, 1020).

| Cancer | Starts from | Share of skin cancers | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Basal cell carcinoma | Stratum basale | Over 75% | 1019, 1020 |
| Squamous cell carcinoma | Upper layers of the epidermis | 20% | 1019, 1020 |
| Melanoma | Melanocytes | Under 5%, but most skin cancer deaths | 1019, 1020 |
| Merkel cell carcinoma | Merkel cells | Rare | 1018, 1019 |

All three main types rise with the UV index (p. 1021). Melanoma 5-year survival is 98% when localized, 62% regional, 15% distant (p. 1019).
