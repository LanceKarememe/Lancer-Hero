## 4. Heart: circuits, valves, flow, auscultation (LO 4)

The heart is two pumps in series; atria receive, ventricles pump away (pp. 42–43).

| Circuit | Leaves the heart from | Via | Blood leaving | Returns via | Returns to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pulmonary | Right ventricle | Pulmonary arteries | Deoxygenated | Pulmonary veins (oxygenated) | Left atrium |
| Systemic | Left ventricle | Systemic arteries | Oxygenated | Systemic veins (deoxygenated) | Right atrium |

The slide's rule: Arteries Always go Away from the heart, whatever the oxygen content (p. 43).

| Valve | Type | Sits between | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## 5. Blood vessels and vascular arrangements (LO 5)

Pressure sets wall thickness: high pressure gives arteries thick elastic walls, low pressure gives veins thin walls (p. 47).

| Vessel | Job | Pressure and wall | Typical blood | Other features |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arteries | Carry blood away from the heart; arterioles deliver it to capillaries | High pressure, thick elastic walls | Oxygen-rich |  |
| Capillaries | Form capillary beds; exchange oxygen, nutrients and waste with extracellular fluid |  |  | Site of exchange |
| Veins | Venules drain the capillaries | Low pressure, thin walls | Low oxygen | Valves, larger diameter |

| Arrangement | What it is | Why it matters | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Anastomosis | Communication between branches of arteries | Collateral circulation: a blocked pathway does not cause ischemia or necrosis | 48 |
| Neurovascular bundle | Artery, vein and nerve wrapped in deep (investing) fascia | Fascia is connective tissue that envelops structures beneath the skin | 48 |
| Venae comitantes | "Accompanying veins" that surround an artery in a branching network and share its fascial sheath | Arteriovenous pump that drives venous blood toward the heart; countercurrent heat exchanger | 48 |

The slide's example of an anastomosis is the genicular (knee) anastomosis (p. 48).

## 6. Lymphatic system (LO 6)

The lymphatic system is an "overflow" system: it returns excess tissue fluid and leaked plasma proteins to the bloodstream and removes debris from cellular decomposition and infection (p. 50). The functions listed on p. 49 are carrying lymph from the interstitial space to the venous system, filtering blood, and initiating an immune response.

| Component | Slide description (p. 50) |
| --- | --- |
| Lymph | Tissue fluid that has entered lymph capillaries; usually clear, watery, slightly yellow |
| Lymphatic capillaries | Thin-walled, blind-ended vessels among the blood capillaries; the entry point of the system |
| Lymphatic vessels | Carry lymph from areas of collection to the venous system |
| Lymph nodes | Small masses of lymph tissue along the vessels; filter lymph on its way to the venous system |

| Vessel group | Location | Receives from | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Superficial | Subcutaneous tissue; converge toward and follow the venous drainage |  | 52 |
| Deep | Accompany arteries | Superficial lymph vessels and internal organs | 52 |

All lymph enters the venous circulation at the root of the neck (p. 52).

| Duct | Drains | Empties at | Formed by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Right lymphatic duct | Right upper quadrant of the body | Right venous angle | Right internal jugular + right subclavian veins |
| Thoracic duct | The remainder of the body | Left venous angle | Left internal jugular + left subclavian veins |

Cancer (p. 53): lymphogenous spread is the most common route for the initial dissemination of carcinomas. Nodes trap the cells and become secondary (metastatic) sites. Involvement follows the natural drainage route, so surgeons stage by removing nodes in the order lymph passes through them.

## 7. Nervous system divisions and neurons (LO 7)

The nervous system is split two ways: by structure and by function (pp. 55–56).

| Division | Made of / carries |
| --- | --- |
| Central nervous system (CNS) | Brain and spinal cord |
| Peripheral nervous system (PNS) | Cranial nerves and spinal nerves |
| Afferent (sensory) | Impulses from the periphery to the CNS |
| Efferent (motor) | Impulses from the CNS to the periphery |
| Somatic | Voluntary; CNS to skeletal muscle |
| Autonomic (ANS) | Involuntary visceral motor; CNS to cardiac muscle, smooth muscle and glands |
| Sympathetic | "Fight or flight" |
| Parasympathetic | "Rest and digest" |
| Enteric | Network of neurons in the wall of the gastrointestinal (GI) tract |

| Neuron term | Slide definition (p. 57) |
| --- | --- |
| Nucleus | Collection of neuronal cell bodies in the CNS |
| Ganglion | Collection of neuronal cell bodies in the PNS |
| Dendrites | Receive information |
| Axon | Sends information |
| Multipolar neuron | Most common type; in the CNS and the motor division of the PNS |
| Pseudounipolar neuron | In sensory ganglia, such as dorsal root ganglia |

| Feature | Cranial nerves | Spinal nerves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Number | 12 pairs | 31 pairs |
| Origin | The brain (mostly) | Segmentally from the spinal cord |
| Route or naming | Pass through openings in the skull | Letter (C, T, L, S, Co) plus number |

Numbering rule (p. 59): cervical nerves are numbered above the corresponding vertebra; all remaining nerves are numbered below it.

One nerve can carry fibers with different functions. The phrenic nerve is motor to the diaphragm and sensory to the fibrous pericardium and parietal pleura (p. 58).

## 9. Somatic vs autonomic; sympathetic vs parasympathetic (LO 9)

The autonomic nervous system (ANS) is a two-neuron motor system: a pre-ganglionic neuron, a ganglion, then a post-ganglionic neuron to the effector (p. 67).

| Feature | Somatic | Autonomic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supplies | All parts of the body except viscera, smooth muscle and glands | Viscera, smooth muscle and glands |
| Sensory side | Touch, temperature, pain from the periphery |  |
| Motor target | Skeletal muscle | Cardiac muscle, smooth muscle, glands |
| Page | 64 | 65 |

| Feature | Sympathetic | Parasympathetic | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Outflow | Thoraco-lumbar: lateral horn, T1–L2 | Cranial-sacral: cranial nerves III, VII, IX, X and S2–S4 | 70–71, 78 |
| Pre-ganglionic fiber | Short | Long | 70 |
| Post-ganglionic fiber | Long | Short | 70 |
| Ganglia | Sympathetic trunk or prevertebral | Cranial or intramural | 70, 72, 78 |
| Pupil | Dilation | Constriction | 66 |
| Heart rate | Increases | Decreases | 66 |
| Coronary vessels | Vasodilation | Vasoconstriction | 66 |
| Bronchi | Bronchodilation | Bronchoconstriction | 66 |
| GI tract | Inhibits activity | Stimulates; intestinal vasodilation | 66 |
| Other | Systemic vasoconstriction, sweating | Salivation, lacrimation | 66 |

| Ganglion type | Location | Slide example (pp. 67–68) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paravertebral | Close to the spinal cord | Sympathetic trunk |
| Prevertebral | Along the midline in the abdomen | Celiac, superior mesenteric |
| Intrinsic (intramural) | On or within the organ innervated | GI tract, heart |
| Cranial | In the head, with cranial nerves | "COPS" ganglia |

COPS is not spelled out on the slide: ciliary, otic, pterygopalatine, submandibular (outside the slides).

| Ramus communicans | Fibers | Slide nickname | Found at | Page |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| White | Pre-ganglionic | "On-ramps" | T1–L2 only | 69 |
| Gray | Post-ganglionic | "Off-ramps" | Entire trunk | 69 |

Every sympathetic pre-ganglionic fiber starts the same way: lateral horn, ventral root, spinal nerve, white ramus communicans, sympathetic trunk. It then takes one of four paths (pp. 73–77):

1. Synapse at the level of entry
2. Ascend the trunk, then synapse
3. Descend the trunk, then synapse
4. Exit the trunk without synapsing there, as a splanchnic nerve, and synapse in a prevertebral ganglion

The prevertebral ganglia named on p. 72 are the celiac, superior mesenteric, aorticorenal and inferior mesenteric. The trunk ganglia are cervical, thoracic, lumbar, sacral and the ganglion impar.

## 12. Pairs you are likely to swap

You tend to swap paired terms, so these are the ones to say out loud before the exam.

| Pair | Keep straight | Page |
| --- | --- | --- |
